Forest Plot

The forest axis is always logarithmic. Choose original values, natural-log values, or both for the numerical effect and 95% CI columns beside it.

HR conversions assume proportional hazards. Applying this formula to SEER cumulative risk with competing deaths is an approximation. IRR is treated as HR under a constant-rate approximation. OR conversions use risk–odds algebra; combining an adjusted OR with an external baseline also gives an approximation.

HR → RR: [1 − (1 − p₀)HR] / p₀
RR → HR: log(1 − p₀ × RR) / log(1 − p₀)
OR → RR: OR / (1 − p₀ + p₀ × OR)
RR → OR: RR × (1 − p₀) / (1 − p₀ × RR)

An unstated exposure direction is assumed to be high versus low, or yes versus no for a binary exposure. This assumption is marked in the study details and does not by itself exclude an otherwise eligible study from pooling.

Without a matching baseline, HR ≈ RR ≈ OR is explicitly treated as an identity approximation. Values implying a probability outside 0–100% cannot be converted. Study eligibility remains Moderate or Good JBI quality.

Sample size calculation

Prospective calculations compare event risks using the chosen baseline. Case-control calculations use OR and compare control and case exposure prevalence: q₁ = OR × q₀ / (1 − q₀ + OR × q₀), with percentages converted to probabilities.

The default exposure percentage is 50%, with one control per case. Case-control sample size does not require a disease-incidence input; any HR/RR-to-OR approximation is identified.
