Forest Plot

The forest axis is always logarithmic. Choose original values, natural-log values, or both for the numerical effect and 95% CI columns beside it.

HR conversions assume proportional hazards: risk is approximated using the baseline event-free probability. Applying this formula to SEER cumulative risk with competing deaths is an approximation. IRR is treated as HR under the existing constant-rate approximation. OR conversions use risk–odds algebra; an adjusted OR combined with an external baseline also gives an approximation.

HR → RR: [1 − (1 − p₀)HR] / p₀
RR → HR: log(1 − p₀ × RR) / log(1 − p₀)
OR → RR: OR / (1 − p₀ + p₀ × OR)
RR → OR: RR × (1 − p₀) / (1 − p₀ × RR)

An unstated exposure direction is assumed to be high versus low, or yes versus no for a binary exposure. This direction assumption is marked in the study details and does not by itself exclude an otherwise eligible study from pooling.

Without a matching baseline, HR ≈ RR ≈ OR is explicitly treated as an identity approximation. Values implying a probability outside 0–100% cannot be converted. Study eligibility remains Moderate or Good JBI quality.

Sample size calculation

Prospective calculations compare event risks using the chosen baseline. Retrospective calculations use OR and compare control and case exposure prevalence: q₁ = OR × q₀ / (1 − q₀ + OR × q₀), with percentages converted to probabilities. The default exposure percentage is 50%. Change the case:control ratio (default 1:1), study design and exposure percentage beside the sample-size result on the main page. Case-control sample size does not require a disease-incidence input; any HR/RR-to-OR approximation is identified.
